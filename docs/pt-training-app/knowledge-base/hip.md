# Hip — Exercise Knowledge Base
Extraction date: 2026-08-03. Sourcing rules: see knowledge-base/README.md.
Corroborated exercises: 25. Candidates: 9.

Pain group scope: hip pain, hip osteoarthritis, hip flexor tightness, glute weakness, and general hip strengthening/mobility. General guidance repeated across Tier 1 sources: warm up 5–10 minutes (walking or easy cycling) before exercising; expect mild, short-lived symptom increase when starting; keep discomfort in the low range and back off if pain is sharp or lingers; strengthening 2–3 days/week, stretching most days; seek professional advice if pain has not improved after about 6 weeks.

## Stretches & Mobility

### Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch
- **Aliases:** half-kneeling hip flexor stretch, kneeling lunge stretch, psoas stretch, low lunge
- **Position:** half-kneeling (one knee on floor)
- **Equipment:** none (folded towel or cushion under the knee is helpful)
- **Target:** hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris) of the rear leg
- **Purpose:** Lengthens hip flexors that shorten with prolonged sitting; tight hip flexors tilt the pelvis forward and contribute to front-of-hip and low-back discomfort. A staple in hip OA and desk-worker mobility programs.
- **Steps:**
  1. Kneel on one knee with the other foot flat on the floor in front, front knee bent about 90 degrees.
  2. Place a folded towel under the down knee for comfort.
  3. Tighten your abdominal and buttock muscles slightly to keep the pelvis level and back straight.
  4. Shift your hips gently forward until you feel a stretch across the front of the hip and thigh of the kneeling leg.
  5. Hold, breathing normally, then ease back.
  6. Switch legs and repeat.
- **Form cues:** stay tall through the trunk — do not lean forward at the waist; tuck the tailbone slightly (posterior pelvic tilt) before shifting forward; keep the front knee over the ankle.
- **Common mistakes:** arching the low back instead of moving the hips forward; lunging so far the front knee passes well beyond the toes; shrugging or holding breath.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 30 seconds, 2–4 repetitions per side, daily or most days.
- **Progression:** raise the back foot onto a low support or reach the same-side arm overhead | **Regression:** do a standing version — stagger stance, tuck the pelvis, and shift weight to the front foot.
- **Cautions:** avoid if kneeling is painful (use the standing version); ease off if you feel pinching in the front of the hip rather than a stretch.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — sagittal-plane kneeling stretch; the forward hip shift is best judged side-on and is too subtle for front-view landmarks.
- **Sources:** [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/stretching/art-20546848) (T1); [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/psoas-stretch-guide-for-psoas-release) (T1); [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthy-aging-and-longevity/do-you-spend-most-of-your-day-sitting-these-hip-flexor-stretches-are-for-you) (T1); [HSS](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/hip-flexor-stretch) (T1); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/pain-relief/exercises-for-hip-pain) (T3); [AskDoctorJo](https://www.askdoctorjo.com/hip-arthritis-stretches-exercises/) (T3)

### Butterfly Stretch
- **Aliases:** seated butterfly, inner thigh stretch, groin stretch, bound angle
- **Position:** seated (floor)
- **Equipment:** none (cushion under hips optional)
- **Target:** hip adductors (inner thigh), groin; opens hip external rotation
- **Purpose:** Loosens the inner thigh and groin muscles that limit how far the hips can open, easing stiffness for activities like getting in and out of a car. Frequently listed in hip-pain and arthritis mobility routines.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit on the floor with your back tall.
  2. Bend both knees and bring the soles of your feet together.
  3. Let your knees fall out to the sides like butterfly wings.
  4. Hold your ankles or shins and draw the heels comfortably toward your body.
  5. For more stretch, hinge slightly forward from the hips with a straight back, or gently press the thighs toward the floor.
  6. Hold, then release.
- **Form cues:** sit tall on the sit bones — avoid slumping; let gravity lower the knees rather than forcing them; hinge from the hips, not the waist.
- **Common mistakes:** rounding the back to get the head low; bouncing the knees aggressively; pulling the feet in so close that the knees pinch.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 20–30 seconds, repeat 2–4 times, daily.
- **Progression:** bring heels closer and add a gentle forward lean | **Regression:** sit on a cushion or against a wall, or keep the feet farther from the body.
- **Cautions:** keep it gentle if you have groin irritation; stop if you feel sharp inner-thigh or front-of-hip pain.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-hold` — seated facing the camera, the knee-drop position is checkable (knee height relative to hip landmarks and the ankle–knee–hip triangle in the frontal plane).
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/hip-opening-stretches) (T1); [Arthritis Foundation](https://www.arthritis.org/health-wellness/healthy-living/physical-activity/getting-started/your-exercise-solution/lower-body-movements/hips-and-buttocks-movements) (T1); [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/exercising-with-arthritis/exercises-for-healthy-joints/exercises-for-the-hips/) (T1); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/325029) (T3); [Spire Healthcare](https://www.spirehealthcare.com/health-hub/specialties/bones-and-joints/the-best-exercises-and-stretches-for-hip-pain/) (T3)

### Figure-4 Piriformis Stretch
- **Aliases:** supine piriformis stretch, figure-four stretch, seated rotation stretch (seated variant), crossed-ankle glute stretch
- **Position:** supine (seated variant available)
- **Equipment:** none
- **Target:** piriformis and deep gluteal rotators, outer hip/buttock
- **Purpose:** Releases the deep buttock muscles that get tight with hip pain and prolonged sitting and that can irritate the sciatic region. Core stretch in the AAOS hip conditioning program and piriformis routines.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back with both knees bent, feet flat.
  2. Cross the ankle of the target leg over the opposite knee, making a "4" shape.
  3. Reach through and grasp the back of the supporting thigh with both hands.
  4. Pull that thigh gently toward your chest until you feel a stretch deep in the buttock of the crossed leg.
  5. Keep head and shoulders relaxed on the floor; hold.
  6. Lower slowly and switch sides.
  7. Seated variant: sit tall in a chair, cross ankle over the opposite knee, and hinge slightly forward.
- **Form cues:** keep the crossed knee falling comfortably outward; keep hips square on the floor; pull with the arms, not by lifting the head and shoulders.
- **Common mistakes:** yanking the leg in fast; letting the pelvis twist off the floor; holding breath.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 20–30 seconds, 2–4 repetitions per side, daily or after activity.
- **Progression:** draw the thigh closer or use the seated version with a deeper forward hinge | **Regression:** rest the supporting foot on a wall instead of holding the thigh.
- **Cautions:** skip the deep version after recent hip surgery unless cleared; ease off if you feel knee strain in the crossed leg.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — floor-lying pose with limb overlap; the seated-chair variant is partially checkable but the stretch itself is too subtle for reliable front-view landmarks.
- **Sources:** [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1); [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/piriformis-syndrome-stretches-exercises) (T1); [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/pain/3-do-at-home-stretches-to-release-hip-and-buttock-pain) (T1); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/pain-relief/exercises-for-hip-pain) (T3)

### Knee-to-Chest Stretch
- **Aliases:** single knee hug, knee pull
- **Position:** supine
- **Equipment:** none
- **Target:** gluteals, low back, posterior hip capsule
- **Purpose:** Gently restores hip flexion range and relieves stiffness through the buttock and lower back — an easy first stretch for painful or arthritic hips.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back with legs relaxed, one leg straight.
  2. Bend the other knee and grasp behind the thigh or over the shin.
  3. Draw the knee smoothly toward your chest until you feel a comfortable stretch in the buttock and back of the hip.
  4. Keep the other leg and your head resting down.
  5. Hold, then slowly lower.
  6. Repeat on the other side.
- **Form cues:** move to the point of stretch, not pain; keep the straight leg pressed gently toward the floor; shoulders and neck relaxed.
- **Common mistakes:** lifting the head to meet the knee; letting the opposite leg hitch up; bouncing the knee.
- **Typical dosage:** hold about 10–30 seconds, 3–5 repetitions per side, 1–3 times daily.
- **Progression:** hug both knees together for a deeper posterior stretch | **Regression:** use a towel looped behind the thigh, or reduce how far you pull.
- **Cautions:** those with a hip replacement should keep within their advised flexion range; stop if it causes a sharp groin pinch.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — supine floor exercise; landmarks are foreshortened from a front camera view.
- **Sources:** [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/exercising-with-arthritis/exercises-for-healthy-joints/exercises-for-the-hips/) (T1); [Arthritis Foundation](https://www.arthritis.org/health-wellness/healthy-living/physical-activity/getting-started/your-exercise-solution/lower-body-movements/hips-and-buttocks-movements) (T1); [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1)

### Supine Hip Rotation Stretch (Knee Rolls)
- **Aliases:** double hip rotation, knees side-to-side, knee drops, lower-trunk rotation
- **Position:** supine
- **Equipment:** none
- **Target:** hip internal/external rotators, outer hip, lower back
- **Purpose:** Restores rotational range of the hip joints, which is often the first movement lost in hip osteoarthritis, while gently mobilizing the low back.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back with both knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart.
  2. Keep shoulders resting on the floor.
  3. Slowly lower both knees together toward one side as far as comfortable.
  4. Turn your head gently the opposite way if comfortable.
  5. Hold briefly, then bring the knees back to center.
  6. Repeat toward the other side.
  7. Easier single-leg variant: let one knee drop out to the side and back while the other stays upright.
- **Form cues:** move slowly and let gravity do the work; keep both shoulders in contact with the floor; keep feet resting on the surface.
- **Common mistakes:** forcing the knees to the floor; rolling the whole trunk over; moving fast and using momentum.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 20–30 seconds per side (or perform 5–10 slow reps side to side), 1–3 times daily.
- **Progression:** hold longer at the end range | **Regression:** place a pillow under the knees to shorten the range, or do one knee at a time.
- **Cautions:** keep the range small if you have low-back irritation; stop if you feel groin pinching.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — supine floor movement viewed edge-on from a front camera; rotation angle is not resolvable.
- **Sources:** [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/exercising-with-arthritis/exercises-for-healthy-joints/exercises-for-the-hips/) (T1); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/325029) (T3); [Spire Healthcare](https://www.spirehealthcare.com/health-hub/specialties/bones-and-joints/the-best-exercises-and-stretches-for-hip-pain/) (T3)

### Standing Iliotibial Band Stretch
- **Aliases:** standing ITB stretch, crossed-leg side lean, hip stretch at the wall
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** wall (for support)
- **Target:** iliotibial band, tensor fasciae latae, outer hip/thigh
- **Purpose:** Eases tightness along the outer hip and thigh that contributes to lateral hip pain and snapping sensations; included in the AAOS hip conditioning program.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand beside a wall with the affected hip nearest the wall, hand on the wall for support.
  2. Cross the leg nearest the wall behind the other leg.
  3. Keep both feet grounded and the trunk tall.
  4. Lean your hips toward the wall until you feel a stretch along the outside of the crossed-back hip.
  5. Hold, then return upright.
  6. Turn around and repeat on the other side.
- **Form cues:** push the hip sideways rather than bending deeply at the waist; keep the back knee straight but not locked; stay square — avoid twisting the trunk.
- **Common mistakes:** simply side-bending the spine without shifting the hips; crossing the wrong leg behind; letting the stretch-side foot roll.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 30 seconds, 2–4 repetitions per side, daily or after activity.
- **Progression:** add an overhead reach with the arm on the stretch side | **Regression:** shorten the lean or hold the wall with both hands.
- **Cautions:** mild pulling only — stop if the outer hip feels sharply painful or the sensation runs down the leg.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-hold` — standing frontal-plane pose; the crossed-leg stance and lateral trunk/pelvis lean (shoulder–hip–ankle line) are visible to a front camera.
- **Sources:** [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1); [Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/exercise-and-fitness/preventing-and-treating-iliotibial-it-band-syndrome-tips-for-pain-free-movement) (T1)

### Supine Hamstring Stretch with Strap
- **Aliases:** lying hamstring stretch, towel hamstring stretch
- **Position:** supine
- **Equipment:** towel (or belt/strap)
- **Target:** hamstrings, posterior hip and thigh
- **Purpose:** Tight hamstrings limit pelvic motion and add strain around the hip; this supported stretch improves flexibility without loading the joint, and is part of standard hip conditioning programs.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back, one leg flat or bent with foot on the floor.
  2. Loop a towel or strap around the sole of the other foot.
  3. Keeping that knee straight, raise the leg by pulling gently on the towel.
  4. Lift until you feel a comfortable pull along the back of the thigh.
  5. Hold, breathing steadily, then lower slowly.
  6. Repeat with the other leg.
- **Form cues:** keep the pelvis and opposite leg down; pull with the arms, keeping the raised knee straight but not locked; keep the neck relaxed.
- **Common mistakes:** bending the knee to get the leg higher; lifting the hips off the floor; jerking the leg upward.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 20–30 seconds, 2–4 repetitions per leg, daily.
- **Progression:** raise the leg higher or walk hands up the strap | **Regression:** bend the resting leg, or stretch with the heel on a low support instead.
- **Cautions:** avoid if it reproduces nerve-type pain (electric/shooting down the leg); keep the pull gentle.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — supine floor stretch; leg elevation angle is not reliably visible from a front-facing camera.
- **Sources:** [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1); [The Orthopedic Clinic](https://orthotoc.com/hip-arthritis-exercises/) (T3); [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/osteoarthritis/knee-and-hip-exercises-for-osteoarthritis) (T3)

### 90/90 Hip Stretch
- **Aliases:** 90-90 stretch, shin box stretch
- **Position:** seated (floor)
- **Equipment:** none
- **Target:** hip external rotators (front leg) and internal rotators (rear leg), glutes, hip capsule
- **Purpose:** Trains both internal and external hip rotation at once — the rotational ranges most restricted in stiff or arthritic hips — improving overall hip mobility for walking and turning.
- **Steps:**
  1. Sit on the floor with the front leg bent 90 degrees in front of you, shin roughly parallel to your hips.
  2. Position the back leg bent 90 degrees out to the side, shin pointing behind you.
  3. Sit tall with both sit bones as close to the floor as possible.
  4. Hinge gently forward over the front shin from the hips until you feel a stretch in the front-leg buttock.
  5. Hold, return upright.
  6. To bias the rear hip, stay upright and press the rear knee/ankle gently toward the floor.
  7. Switch leg positions and repeat.
- **Form cues:** keep the chest tall and hinge from the hips; both knees stay near 90 degrees; keep the front foot flexed to protect the knee.
- **Common mistakes:** collapsing the trunk into a slump; letting the pelvis lift high off the floor on one side; forcing rotation through the knee instead of the hip.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 30–60 seconds per side, 2–3 repetitions, several times per week.
- **Progression:** transition slowly between sides without using hands ("90/90 switches") | **Regression:** sit on a cushion or support yourself with both hands behind you.
- **Cautions:** keep range comfortable if you have knee issues or hip impingement-type pinching; this one is moderately advanced — skip early in rehab.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-hold` — seated upright facing the camera, the windmill leg configuration and upright-vs-hinged trunk are checkable, though shin angles may be partially foreshortened.
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/90-90-stretch) (T1); [E3 Rehab](https://e3rehab.com/how-to-improve-your-hip-range-of-motion/) (T3); [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/hip-opening-stretches) (T3)

### Pigeon Pose
- **Aliases:** pigeon stretch, sleeping pigeon, figure-4 on the floor
- **Position:** quadruped to floor-lying
- **Equipment:** none (cushion under hip optional)
- **Target:** glutes and deep external rotators of the front hip; hip flexors of the rear leg
- **Purpose:** A deep hip opener that stretches the buttock of the front leg and the hip flexors of the back leg simultaneously — useful for stubborn outer-hip and buttock tightness in people who tolerate floor work.
- **Steps:**
  1. Start on hands and knees.
  2. Bring one knee forward and place it behind the same-side wrist, shin angled across the body.
  3. Slide the other leg straight back, top of the foot on the floor.
  4. Square the hips toward the floor as much as comfortable.
  5. Stay tall on the hands, or fold the chest forward over the front shin for a deeper stretch.
  6. Hold, then push back to hands-and-knees and switch sides.
- **Form cues:** keep hips level — support the front-side hip with a cushion if it hovers; front foot flexed; lengthen the back leg straight behind, not out to the side.
- **Common mistakes:** letting the body tilt onto one hip; forcing the front shin fully parallel; dumping weight into the front knee.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 30–60 seconds per side, 1–3 repetitions.
- **Progression:** fold the torso fully forward (sleeping pigeon) | **Regression:** do the supine figure-4 stretch instead, or keep the chest upright with hands supporting.
- **Cautions:** advanced stretch — avoid with significant knee problems, after hip replacement, or if it causes groin pinching; choose the figure-4 version when unsure.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — low floor pose with overlapping limbs; not resolvable from a phone front camera.
- **Sources:** [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/hip-opening-stretches) (T1); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/pain-relief/exercises-for-hip-pain) (T3); [Yoga Journal](https://www.yogajournal.com/practice/yoga-sequences/hip-openers-besides-pigeon/) (T3)

## Strengthening

### Glute Bridge
- **Aliases:** bridging, pelvic lift, hip bridge
- **Position:** supine
- **Equipment:** none (resistance band above knees optional)
- **Target:** gluteus maximus, hamstrings, core/pelvic stabilizers
- **Purpose:** The most widely recommended hip strengthening exercise: builds the glutes that support and unload the hip joint, while gently opening the front of the hips — all without joint compression from standing.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width apart, arms by your sides.
  2. Gently tighten your abdominals and squeeze your buttocks.
  3. Push through your heels and lift your hips until shoulders, hips, and knees form a straight line.
  4. Hold 2–5 seconds at the top, breathing normally.
  5. Lower slowly back to the floor with control.
  6. Repeat.
- **Form cues:** squeeze the glutes to lift, don't arch the low back; keep knees tracking over the feet, not falling in or out; ribs down, avoid over-lifting.
- **Common mistakes:** pushing so high the back hyperextends; lifting with the hamstrings only (cramping); letting knees splay.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 8–15 reps (or 5-second holds), 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** band above knees, single-leg bridge, or feet on a step | **Regression:** smaller lift height, or hold a static low bridge.
- **Cautions:** keep the range small if it triggers back or buttock cramping; stop with any sharp pain.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — supine floor exercise; hip elevation is edge-on to a front camera and unreliable for landmark tracking.
- **Sources:** [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-hip-problems/) (T1); [Sussex Community NHS](https://www.sussexcommunity.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/resources/patient-resources/hip-osteoarthritis/rehabilitation-exercises) (T1); [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/hip-opening-stretches) (T1); [GLA:D program](https://gladinternational.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/NEMEX-Training-Program.pdf) (T1); [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Therapy_Exercises_for_the_Hip) (T2); [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/49/glute-bridge/) (T2); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/pain-relief/exercises-for-hip-pain) (T3)

### Clamshell
- **Aliases:** clam, side-lying clam, clam with band
- **Position:** side-lying
- **Equipment:** none (resistance band above knees to progress)
- **Target:** gluteus medius, deep hip external rotators
- **Purpose:** Isolates the outer-hip stabilizers that keep the pelvis level when walking and climbing stairs; weakness here is strongly linked to lateral hip pain and poor knee tracking.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your side with hips and knees bent about 45–90 degrees, legs stacked, heels together.
  2. Rest your head on your arm; keep hips stacked vertically.
  3. Keeping the feet touching, lift the top knee up like a clam opening.
  4. Lift only as far as you can without the pelvis rolling backward.
  5. Pause briefly, then lower the knee slowly.
  6. Complete the set, then switch sides.
- **Form cues:** imagine your top hip pinned so only the thigh rotates; keep feet together throughout; slow and controlled both directions.
- **Common mistakes:** rolling the pelvis backward to fake more range; lifting the feet with the knee; going too fast.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps per side, 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add a resistance band above the knees, or lift the feet as well | **Regression:** reduce the lift height or reps.
- **Cautions:** if it aggravates a tender outer hip (e.g., irritated lateral tendons), reduce range or swap for another abductor exercise; mild muscle burn is expected.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — side-lying floor exercise; knee elevation is largely occluded/foreshortened for a front camera.
- **Sources:** [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-greater-trochanteric-pain-syndrome/) (T1); [Arthritis Foundation](https://www.arthritis.org/health-wellness/healthy-living/physical-activity/getting-started/your-exercise-solution/lower-body-movements/hips-and-buttocks-movements) (T1); [Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Therapy_Exercises_for_the_Hip) (T2); [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/resources/articles/clamshell/) (T3); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/fitness-exercise/the-clamshell-exercise) (T3)

### Side-Lying Leg Raise
- **Aliases:** side-lying hip abduction, lying side leg lift
- **Position:** side-lying
- **Equipment:** none (ankle weight to progress; pillow between knees for setup comfort)
- **Target:** gluteus medius and minimus (hip abductors), outer thigh
- **Purpose:** Classic abductor strengthener that stabilizes the pelvis in single-leg stance; consistently recommended for hip OA, lateral hip pain, and glute weakness.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your side, bottom leg bent slightly for balance, top leg straight and in line with your body.
  2. Support your head; stack hips vertically.
  3. Tighten the front-of-thigh muscles of the top leg to keep the knee straight.
  4. Lift the top leg toward the ceiling about 20–30 cm (roughly 45 degrees or less).
  5. Hold 2–6 seconds, then lower slowly with control.
  6. Complete the set, then switch sides.
- **Form cues:** keep the leg in line with the trunk — do not let it drift forward; toes point forward, not upward; lift from the hip, keeping the waist long.
- **Common mistakes:** rolling the pelvis backward and lifting with the hip flexors; hiking the hip toward the ribs; swinging the leg with momentum.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps per side (Kaiser: hold ~6 seconds each), 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add an ankle weight or resistance band | **Regression:** shorten the lift or bend the top knee slightly.
- **Cautions:** if lying on the bottom hip is painful, pad the surface or choose the standing version; keep range pain-free.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — side-lying on the floor; the abduction arc points toward the ceiling and is poorly resolvable from a front camera.
- **Sources:** [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1); [Kaiser Permanente](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/health-wellness/health-encyclopedia/he.hip-arthritis-exercises.bo1540) (T1); [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-hip-problems/) (T1); [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/38/side-lying-hip-abduction/) (T2); [HSS](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/hip-strengthening-exercises) (T1); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/hip-exercises) (T3)

### Standing Hip Abduction
- **Aliases:** standing side leg lift, side leg raise at chair
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** chair or wall for support (band around ankles to progress)
- **Target:** gluteus medius/minimus (hip abductors)
- **Purpose:** Strengthens the outer-hip muscles in an upright, functional position, improving pelvic control for walking; easier to set up than floor versions and commonly prescribed for hip OA.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall beside or behind a chair, one hand resting on it for balance.
  2. Shift weight onto the supporting leg.
  3. Keeping the moving knee straight and toes pointing forward, lift the other leg straight out to the side.
  4. Hold 2–5 seconds at the top.
  5. Lower slowly back to the start without letting the foot rest between reps if able.
  6. Complete the set, then switch sides.
- **Form cues:** keep the trunk upright — don't lean away to lift higher; keep toes and kneecap facing forward; move slow and controlled.
- **Common mistakes:** side-bending the trunk to hoist the leg; turning the toes up/outward (using hip flexors); swinging with momentum.
- **Typical dosage:** 5–10 reps per side building to 2–3 sets of 10–12, daily to 3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add a resistance band around the ankles or light ankle weight; reduce hand support | **Regression:** smaller lift, keep both hands supported.
- **Cautions:** mild outer-hip muscle burn is normal; stop if joint pain rises above a comfortable level or you feel unsteady.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — frontal-plane motion ideal for a front camera; primary signal: hip abduction angle (trunk–hip–ankle of the moving leg), plus trunk-lean check via shoulder–hip line.
- **Sources:** [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-osteoarthritis-of-the-hip) (T1); [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/conditions/hip-pain/) (T1); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/pain-relief/exercises-for-hip-pain) (T3)

### Standing Hip Extension
- **Aliases:** standing leg lift to the back, standing kickback
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** chair or wall for support
- **Target:** gluteus maximus, hamstrings
- **Purpose:** Strengthens the hip extensors that power walking, stairs, and standing up, in a joint-friendly upright position — a standard NHS exercise for hip pain and OA.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall holding a chair back or work surface.
  2. Keep the moving knee straight and the trunk upright.
  3. Squeeze the buttock and lift one leg straight behind you a comfortable distance.
  4. Hold 2–3 seconds.
  5. Lower slowly to the start.
  6. Complete the set, then switch legs.
- **Form cues:** keep the back straight — the movement comes from the hip, not the low back; stay tall over the standing leg; squeeze the glute to move the leg.
- **Common mistakes:** leaning the chest forward and arching the back to lift higher; bending the moving knee; rushing the reps.
- **Typical dosage:** 5–10 reps per side, building toward 2–3 sets, daily to 3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add ankle weight or a band; slow the lowering phase | **Regression:** smaller range; keep toes lightly touching the floor between reps.
- **Cautions:** avoid arching the low back; keep range small if you feel back or front-of-hip pinching.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — the leg travels backward away from a front camera and is mostly occluded by the body; range is too subtle for reliable front-view joint angles.
- **Sources:** [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-hip-problems/) (T1); [RJAH NHS](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/hip-osteoarthritis/) (T1)

### Prone Straight-Leg Raise
- **Aliases:** prone hip extension, lying face-down leg lift
- **Position:** prone
- **Equipment:** none (pillow under hips optional)
- **Target:** gluteus maximus, hamstrings
- **Purpose:** Builds the glutes with the hip fully supported, teaching buttock activation without back strain — used across arthritis and hip conditioning programs.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie face down, legs straight, forehead resting on your hands.
  2. Gently tighten your stomach and both buttocks.
  3. Keeping the knee straight, lift one leg slightly off the floor (a few inches is enough).
  4. Keep both hip bones pressing into the floor.
  5. Hold 5–10 seconds, breathing normally.
  6. Lower slowly; repeat, then switch legs.
- **Form cues:** lift from the buttock, not the low back; keep hips level on the floor; small lift — height is not the goal.
- **Common mistakes:** lifting so high the pelvis twists or the back arches; bending the knee; holding breath.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 5–10 seconds, about 3–8 reps per side, 1–2 times daily (Versus Arthritis: hold 5–10 s, repeat 3).
- **Progression:** add reps/hold time or a light ankle weight | **Regression:** place a pillow under the hips, or just tense the buttock without lifting.
- **Cautions:** skip if lying on your front is uncomfortable (e.g., breathing or back issues); keep the lift small to protect the low back.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — prone floor exercise, invisible to a phone front camera at usable angles.
- **Sources:** [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/exercising-with-arthritis/exercises-for-healthy-joints/exercises-for-the-hips/) (T1); [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1)

### Donkey Kick
- **Aliases:** quadruped bent-knee hip extension, glute kickback
- **Position:** quadruped
- **Equipment:** none (mat recommended)
- **Target:** gluteus maximus (bent knee biases glutes over hamstrings), core stabilizers
- **Purpose:** Strengthens the glutes through hip extension while the joint is unloaded on hands and knees; the bent knee focuses the work on the buttock, useful for glute weakness behind hip pain.
- **Steps:**
  1. Start on hands and knees, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
  2. Keep the back flat and core braced.
  3. Keeping the knee bent about 90 degrees, squeeze the buttock and lift one foot toward the ceiling.
  4. Raise the thigh until it is about level with the trunk — stop before the back arches.
  5. Lower the knee back toward the floor with control.
  6. Complete the set, then switch legs.
- **Form cues:** move only at the hip — imagine balancing a cup on your low back; sole of the foot faces the ceiling; hips stay square to the floor.
- **Common mistakes:** sagging or arching the low back as the leg rises; rotating the pelvis open; kicking with momentum.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps per side, 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add a band behind the knee, ankle weight, or pulses at the top | **Regression:** smaller lift, or slide the leg back along the floor instead.
- **Cautions:** avoid if kneeling on hands/knees hurts wrists or knees; keep range modest if the low back complains.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — quadruped position is side-profile by nature; from a front view the lifting leg is occluded behind the torso.
- **Sources:** [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/270/quadruped-bent-knee-hip-extensions/) (T2); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/325029) (T3); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/hip-exercises) (T3); [Spire Healthcare](https://www.spirehealthcare.com/health-hub/specialties/bones-and-joints/the-best-exercises-and-stretches-for-hip-pain/) (T3); [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/resources/articles/donkey-kicks/) (T3)

### Straight Leg Raise (Supine)
- **Aliases:** SLR, lying leg raise, knee raise (NHS leaflets)
- **Position:** supine
- **Equipment:** none (ankle weight to progress)
- **Target:** hip flexors and quadriceps; abdominals as stabilizers
- **Purpose:** Strengthens the front of the hip and thigh without bending the painful joint under load, supporting activities like stairs and getting out of chairs.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your back with one knee bent, foot flat, and the other leg straight.
  2. Tighten the thigh of the straight leg so the knee is firm.
  3. Lift the straight leg until the ankle is about level with the opposite bent knee.
  4. Pause briefly at the top.
  5. Lower the leg slowly back to the floor.
  6. Complete the set, then swap legs.
- **Form cues:** keep the low back gently flattened toward the floor; lead with a locked-straight knee; move slowly both directions.
- **Common mistakes:** arching the back as the leg lifts; bending the lifting knee; dropping the leg quickly.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps per side, daily to 3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add a light ankle weight or pause longer at the top | **Regression:** reduce lift height, or start with short holds just off the floor.
- **Cautions:** ease off if it provokes front-of-hip pinching or back pain; keep the working range comfortable.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — supine floor exercise; the lifting leg is foreshortened straight toward a front camera.
- **Sources:** [NHS (Berkshire Healthcare) hip OA leaflet](https://www.berkshirehealthcare.nhs.uk/media/kjbn55ek/osteoarthritis-of-the-hip-physio-leaflet-berkshire-healthcare.pdf) (T1); [Aurora Health Care](https://ahc.aurorahealthcare.org/fywb/x09098.pdf) (T1); [AskDoctorJo](https://www.askdoctorjo.com/hip-arthritis-stretches-exercises/) (T3)

### Mini Squat
- **Aliases:** partial squat, quarter squat, supported squat, knee bends
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** chair, counter, or wall for support
- **Target:** glutes, quadriceps, hip stabilizers
- **Purpose:** Trains the whole lower limb through a small, controlled range that most painful hips tolerate well, restoring the strength needed for stairs and standing tasks.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall with feet hip-width apart, hands resting on a counter or chair back if needed.
  2. Keep the chest up and heels down.
  3. Bend hips and knees to lower a small comfortable amount (knees roughly over toes).
  4. Hold up to a count of five if able.
  5. Push through the heels to return to standing, squeezing the buttocks.
  6. Repeat.
- **Form cues:** send the hips slightly back as you bend; knees track in line with the toes; keep weight even through both feet.
- **Common mistakes:** letting the knees dive inward; going deeper than the comfortable range; leaning heavily on the support.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 5–10 reps, daily to 3 days/week (Versus Arthritis: hold 5 count, repeat ~5).
- **Progression:** squat deeper, remove hand support, or add light load | **Regression:** shallower bend with both hands supported.
- **Cautions:** keep depth within the pain-free range; stop if the hip or knee sharply complains.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — repetitive vertical motion visible from the front; primary signal: knee flexion angle (hip–knee–ankle) plus hip-landmark vertical displacement; can also flag knee valgus.
- **Sources:** [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/conditions/hip-pain/) (T1); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/325029) (T3); [Spire Healthcare](https://www.spirehealthcare.com/health-hub/specialties/bones-and-joints/the-best-exercises-and-stretches-for-hip-pain/) (T3)

### Sit-to-Stand (Chair Squat)
- **Aliases:** chair stand, chair rise, box squat to touch
- **Position:** standing (with chair)
- **Equipment:** chair
- **Target:** glutes, quadriceps, hip extensors; functional lower-limb strength
- **Purpose:** Rebuilds the exact movement most limited by hip pain — rising from a seat — and doubles as a strength and confidence exercise; a core functional exercise in NHS and GLA:D programs.
- **Steps:**
  1. Place a stable chair with its back against a wall; stand in front of it, feet shoulder-width, heels slightly back.
  2. Cross arms over chest or reach them forward.
  3. Hinge at the hips, lean the chest slightly forward, and bend knees to lower slowly toward the seat.
  4. Let your bottom just touch the chair — avoid fully sitting if the goal is reps.
  5. Drive through the heels and stand back up tall, squeezing the glutes.
  6. Repeat with control.
- **Form cues:** nose over toes as you rise; push evenly through both legs; control the lowering — don't drop into the chair.
- **Common mistakes:** plopping down with no control; favoring the stronger leg; using hands on thighs or armrests when not needed.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 5–12 reps, 2–3 days/week (also used as a 30-second self-test).
- **Progression:** lower chair height, slow 3–5 s descent, or hold a light weight | **Regression:** raise the seat with a cushion or push lightly from the armrests.
- **Cautions:** use a stable chair that won't slide; pause if you feel dizzy on standing; keep depth comfortable.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — clear repetitive stand/sit cycle from the front; primary signal: hip-landmark vertical displacement with trunk–hip–knee angle for depth and lean.
- **Sources:** [NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/muscle-bone-and-joints/leg-and-foot-problems-and-conditions/exercises-for-osteoarthritis-of-the-hip) (T1); [GLA:D program](https://gladcanada.ca/glad-exercises/) (T1); [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/325029) (T3); [AskDoctorJo](https://www.askdoctorjo.com/hip-arthritis-stretches-exercises/) (T3)

### Step-Up
- **Aliases:** stair step-up, box step-up, step exercise
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** step (bottom stair or low platform); rail/wall for balance
- **Target:** glutes, quadriceps, hip stabilizers; single-leg control
- **Purpose:** Functional single-leg strengthening that transfers directly to stair climbing and walking, and challenges pelvic stability on the stance leg.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand facing the bottom step of your stairs, hand on the rail if needed.
  2. Step up with the affected/target leg, placing the whole foot on the step.
  3. Push through that leg to bring the other foot up onto the step.
  4. Step back down with the second leg first, then the first.
  5. Continue for the set, then lead with the other leg.
  6. Keep a steady rhythm without pulling on the rail.
- **Form cues:** keep the knee of the stepping leg tracking over the foot; stand tall — avoid pitching the chest forward; push up through the stepping leg rather than launching off the floor leg.
- **Common mistakes:** letting the knee collapse inward; hopping off the back foot; hiking the pelvis on the way up.
- **Typical dosage:** 30 seconds of continuous steps or 8–12 reps per leg, 2–3 sets, 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** taller step, no hand support, or hold light weights; slow lowering | **Regression:** lower step and firm hand support.
- **Cautions:** use a rail until confident in balance; stop if the hip pinches at the top of the step.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — repetitive vertical body displacement visible from the front; primary signal: hip/shoulder landmark height cycles plus stance-knee angle (hip–knee–ankle); pelvis-drop detectable via hip-landmark tilt.
- **Sources:** [Sussex Community NHS](https://www.sussexcommunity.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/resources/patient-resources/hip-osteoarthritis/rehabilitation-exercises) (T1); [GLA:D program](https://gladcanada.ca/glad-exercises/) (T1)

### Forward Lunge
- **Aliases:** static lunge, split squat (static variant)
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** none (chair or wall nearby for balance; light dumbbells to progress)
- **Target:** glutes, quadriceps, hip flexors of the rear leg; balance and control
- **Purpose:** A higher-level functional exercise from the GLA:D neuromuscular program that builds single-leg strength and dynamic hip control while stretching the rear hip flexors.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall, feet hip-width apart, near support if needed.
  2. Take a large controlled step forward with one leg.
  3. Bend both knees to lower the back knee toward the floor as far as comfortable.
  4. Keep the front knee above the ankle and trunk upright.
  5. Push firmly through the front heel to step back to standing.
  6. Alternate legs or complete a set per side.
- **Form cues:** think "down, not forward" — drop the back knee rather than pushing the front knee ahead; keep hips facing forward; take a wide enough step to feel stable.
- **Common mistakes:** short choppy step causing the front knee to shoot past the toes; wobbling from a narrow base; slamming the back knee down.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 5–10 reps per leg, 2–3 days/week; progress gradually.
- **Progression:** add dumbbells, deepen range, or move to walking lunges | **Regression:** shallow range with fingertip support, or a static split-stance hold.
- **Cautions:** an advanced move — build up with sit-to-stands and mini squats first; progress slowly to avoid stirring up the joint.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — repetitive lowering visible from the front; primary signal: hip-landmark vertical displacement plus front-knee flexion angle (hip–knee–ankle); stance width checkable from ankle landmarks.
- **Sources:** [GLA:D program](https://gladinternational.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/NEMEX-Training-Program.pdf) (T1); [AskDoctorJo](https://www.askdoctorjo.com/hip-arthritis-stretches-exercises/) (T3); [Cleveland Clinic (lunge stretch variant)](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/hip-opening-stretches) (T1)

### Lateral Band Walk
- **Aliases:** side-stepping with band, banded lateral walk, monster walk (diagonal variant), crab walk
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** resistance band (loop around thighs or ankles)
- **Target:** gluteus medius and maximus, hip abductors
- **Purpose:** Loads the outer-hip stabilizers in a functional standing pattern; research shows strong gluteus medius activation, making it a go-to for glute weakness and pelvis control during gait.
- **Steps:**
  1. Place a loop band just above the knees (easier) or around the ankles (harder).
  2. Stand with feet hip-width apart, knees and hips slightly bent in a shallow athletic stance.
  3. Keeping tension on the band, step sideways a comfortable distance with the lead foot.
  4. Follow with the trailing foot, returning to hip-width — never let the feet touch.
  5. Take 8–12 steps in one direction, staying level.
  6. Repeat leading in the opposite direction.
- **Form cues:** stay low and level — no bobbing up and down; keep toes pointing forward and knees pushed gently outward; keep constant band tension.
- **Common mistakes:** standing up tall between steps; letting the knees cave inward; dragging the trailing foot so the band goes slack; leaning the trunk toward the step direction.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 8–12 steps each way, 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** move the band to the ankles/forefeet or use a heavier band; adopt a deeper semi-squat | **Regression:** band above knees, lighter band, or side-step without a band.
- **Cautions:** skip the band if it provokes outer-hip tendon pain — plain side-stepping first; keep steps small if balance is uncertain.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — frontal-plane side-stepping is well suited to a front camera; primary signal: inter-ankle distance oscillation with stance width, plus knee alignment (hip–knee–ankle) and level-pelvis check.
- **Sources:** [PMC narrative review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12372021/) (T1); [ISSA](https://www.issaonline.com/blog/post/master-the-lateral-band-walk-for-a-stronger-gluteus-medius) (T2); [ACE Physical Therapy](https://ace-pt.org/ace-physical-therapy-and-sports-medicine-institute-side-stepping-to-treat-low-back-and-lower-extremity-injuries/) (T3)

### Side-Lying Hip Adduction
- **Aliases:** inner-thigh lift, bottom-leg raise, hip adduction lying
- **Position:** side-lying
- **Equipment:** none (ankle weight to progress)
- **Target:** hip adductors (inner thigh)
- **Purpose:** Balances hip strength by working the often-neglected inner thigh, part of the AAOS hip conditioning program for a stable, well-supported hip.
- **Steps:**
  1. Lie on your side with the bottom leg straight.
  2. Cross the top leg over, placing its foot flat on the floor in front of the bottom knee (or rest it on a cushion).
  3. Keep your trunk still and head supported.
  4. Tighten the inner thigh and lift the bottom leg a few inches toward the ceiling.
  5. Pause, then lower slowly without resting fully.
  6. Complete the set, then switch sides.
- **Form cues:** keep the bottom knee straight and toes pulling gently toward you; move only the leg — the waist stays quiet; small controlled lifts.
- **Common mistakes:** rolling the trunk backward; lifting with momentum; letting the bottom leg drift forward.
- **Typical dosage:** 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps per side, 2–3 days/week.
- **Progression:** add an ankle weight (1-lb increments) | **Regression:** smaller range or fewer reps.
- **Cautions:** keep it gentle with groin strains — inner-thigh work can aggravate them; stop with sharp groin pain.
- **Camera trackability:** `not-trackable` — side-lying floor exercise with a small lift of the lower leg; occluded and too subtle for front-view landmarks.
- **Sources:** [AAOS OrthoInfo](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/hip-conditioning-program/) (T1); [ACE](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/39/side-lying-hip-adduction/) (T2); [Northwell Health](https://www.northwell.edu/sites/northwell.edu/files/2020-07/rehab-exercises-for-hip-and-pelvis.pdf) (T1)

## Balance & Stability

### Single-Leg Stance
- **Aliases:** single-leg balance, one-leg stand, standing balance
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** chair, counter, or wall nearby for safety
- **Target:** hip abductors/stabilizers, ankle stabilizers, balance system
- **Purpose:** Trains the hip's stabilizing muscles and balance reactions used in every step; improves pelvic control and lowers fall risk — included in NHS OA rehab and neuromuscular programs.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall next to a counter or sturdy chair, fingertips hovering or lightly touching.
  2. Shift weight fully onto one leg, keeping that knee slightly soft (not locked).
  3. Lift the other foot a few inches off the floor.
  4. Keep the pelvis level and trunk upright; hold the position.
  5. Aim for 20–30 seconds, then lower with control.
  6. Repeat on the other leg.
- **Form cues:** keep hips level — don't let the free-side hip drop; stand tall, eyes forward; quiet foot — minimize wobbling.
- **Common mistakes:** hitching or dropping the pelvis; leaning the trunk over the stance leg; gripping the support the whole time.
- **Typical dosage:** hold 20–30 seconds per leg, 3–5 repetitions, daily.
- **Progression:** no hand support, eyes closed, stand on a cushion, or add small head turns | **Regression:** fingertip support, shorter holds, or just weight-shifting side to side.
- **Cautions:** always have support within reach; avoid on days of significant dizziness or after a fall without guidance.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-hold` — static standing pose facing the camera; checkable via raised-ankle height difference, level-pelvis (hip landmark symmetry), and trunk-vertical line, plus hold-duration timing.
- **Sources:** [Sussex Community NHS](https://www.sussexcommunity.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/resources/patient-resources/hip-osteoarthritis/rehabilitation-exercises) (T1); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/pain-relief/exercises-for-hip-pain) (T3); [ChoosePT](https://www.choosept.com/guide/physical-therapy-guide-to-osteoarthritis-of-hip) (T2)

### Standing March
- **Aliases:** marching on the spot, standing knee raise, standing hip flexion
- **Position:** standing
- **Equipment:** chair or work surface for support
- **Target:** hip flexors, quadriceps; stance-leg stabilizers and balance
- **Purpose:** Strengthens the hip flexors and rehearses the swing phase of walking while challenging single-leg stability — a gentle, functional exercise recommended across arthritis and NHS resources.
- **Steps:**
  1. Stand tall holding a work surface or chair back.
  2. Shift weight to one leg.
  3. Lift the other knee up toward your chest, to about hip height or a comfortable level.
  4. Hold a second or two, then lower the foot with control.
  5. Repeat with the other leg, marching alternately at a steady pace.
  6. Continue for the set, staying upright throughout.
- **Form cues:** stay tall — avoid leaning back or sideways as the knee lifts; lift the knee, not the shoulder; controlled tempo rather than stomping.
- **Common mistakes:** leaning back to hoist the knee higher; letting the stance-side hip collapse; rushing.
- **Typical dosage:** 10 lifts per leg (or 30–60 seconds of marching), 2–3 sets, daily to 3 days/week.
- **Progression:** lighten hand support, raise knees higher, slow the lowering, or march without support | **Regression:** smaller knee lifts with both hands supported, or do it seated (seated knee lift).
- **Cautions:** some therapists advise keeping the knee at or below hip level with hip OA; keep support close if balance is unsteady.
- **Camera trackability:** `trackable-reps` — alternating knee raises are clearly visible from the front; primary signal: hip flexion via knee-landmark height relative to hip landmark (shoulder–hip–knee angle), alternating left/right.
- **Sources:** [Versus Arthritis](https://versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/exercising-with-arthritis/exercises-for-healthy-joints/exercises-for-the-hips/) (T1); [RJAH NHS](https://www.rjah.nhs.uk/our-services/therapy/supported-self-care/hip-osteoarthritis/) (T1); [AskDoctorJo (seated variant)](https://www.askdoctorjo.com/video/5-hip-osteoarthritis-pain-relief-exercises-seated/) (T3)
